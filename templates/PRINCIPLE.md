## 1. Core principle

Explain the principle directly. State the decision it helps make and the priority
or relation that matters. Do not leave the reader to decode a slogan.

## 2. Reasoning

Explain why the principle exists. Show the connection between the problem, the
proposed way of thinking, and its expected consequence. Define necessary technical
terms. Distinguish observed evidence from interpretation and analogy.

## 3. Application

Describe when to use the principle and how it changes a decision. Give a concrete
case with enough context to make the reasoning understandable. Identify whether
the case is recorded or hypothetical. Use steps only when they help.

## 4. Limits and pitfalls

Explain when the principle does not apply, what it can cost, and how it can be
misused. Preserve important exceptions from each source. Do not create universal
rules from one task's settings or infer an unstated motive.

## 5. Related principles

Link only principles that help the reader distinguish or apply this idea. Explain
the relation briefly. A related link is not an instruction to load another file.
If none adds value, write that no related entry is needed.

## 6. Sources and standing

Identify the source and date, retained case or evidence IDs, and any earlier
principle IDs merged here. Link to the relevant evidence or external text. State
whether the explanation is directly stated, inferred, borrowed, or combined.
Keep missing context and tentative scope visible. Do not claim an untested benefit
or classify working guidance as a core value.
